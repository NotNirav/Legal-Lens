Works Committee. 

**3.** ( _1_ ) In the case of any industrial establishment in which one hundred or more workers are employed or have been employed on any day in the preceding twelve months, the appropriate Government may by general or special order require the employer to constitute a Works Committee, in such manner as may be prescribed, consisting of representatives of employer and workers engaged in the establishment:

( _10_ ) Notwithstanding anything contained in this section or section 53, any worker as is specified in sub-section ( _5_ ) may, make an application directly to the Tribunal for adjudication of the dispute referred to therein after the expiry of forty-five days from the date he has made the application to the conciliation officer of the appropriate Government for conciliation of the dispute, and on receipt of such application the Tribunal shall have powers and jurisdiction to adjudicate upon the dispute, as the Tribunal has in respect of the application filed under sub-section ( _6_ ) of section 53.

( _10_ ) An employer who fails to submit draft standing orders as required by section 30, or who modifies his standing orders otherwise than in accordance with section 35, shall be punishable with fine which shall not be less than fifty thousand rupees, but which may extend to two lakh rupees and in the case of a continuing offence with an additional fine of two thousand rupees per day till the offence continues. 

( _11_ ) An employer who does any act in contravention of the standing orders finally certified under this Code shall be punishable with fine which shall not be less than one lakh rupees, but which may extend to two lakh rupees. 

( _12_ ) Any person who after conviction under sub-section ( _11_ ) again commits the same offence, then, he shall, for committing the second or subsequent offence be punishable with fine which shall not be less than two lakh rupees, but which may extend to four lakh rupees or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three months, or with both. 

( _13_ ) Any worker who commences, continues or otherwise acts in furtherance of a strike which is illegal under this Code, shall be punishable with fine which shall not be less than one thousand rupees, but which may extend up to ten thousand rupees or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one month, or with both. 

( _14_ ) Any employer who commences, continues, or otherwise acts in furtherance of a lock-out which is illegal under this Code, shall be punishable with fine which shall not be less than fifty thousand rupees, but which may extend to one lakh rupees or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one month, or with both. 

( _15_ ) Any person who instigates or incites others to take part in, or otherwise acts in furtherance of, a strike or lock-out which is illegal under this Code, shall be punishable with fine which shall not be less than ten thousand rupees, but which may extend to fifty thousand rupees or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one month, or with both. 

( _16_ ) Any person who knowingly spends or applies any money in direct furtherance or support of any illegal strike or lock-out shall be punishable with fine which shall not be less than ten thousand rupees, but which may extend to fifty thousand rupees or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one month, or with both. 

( _17_ ) Any person who commits a breach of any term of any settlement or award, which is binding on him under this Code, shall be punishable with fine which shall not be less than 

42 

twenty thousand rupees, but which may extend to two lakh rupees or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three months, or with both. 

( _18_ ) Where the breach under sub-section ( _17_ ) is a continuing one, the offender shall be punishable with an additional fine which may extend to one thousand rupees for every day during which the breach continues after the first conviction and the court trying the offence, if it fines the offender, may direct that the whole or any part of the fine realised from him shall be paid, by way of compensation, to any person who, in its opinion, has been affected by such breach. 

( _19_ ) Any person who wilfully discloses any such information as is referred to in section 61 in contravention of the provisions of that section shall, on a complaint made by or on behalf of the Trade Union or individual business affected, be punishable with fine which may extend to twenty thousand rupees, or with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one month, or with both. 

( _20_ ) Any person who contravenes any other provision of this Code not covered under sub-sections ( _1_ ) to ( _19_ ) or the rules or regulations framed under this Code shall be punishable with fine which may extend to one lakh rupees. 

Cognizance of offences. 

**87.** ( _1_ ) No court shall take cognizance of any offence punishable under this Code, save on a complaint made by or under the authority of the appropriate Government. 

( _2_ ) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, no 2 of 1974. court inferior to that of the Metropolitan Magistrate or Judicial Magistrate of the first class shall try the offences under this Code. 

Offences by companies. 

**88.** ( _1_ ) If the person committing an offence under this Code is a company, every person who, at the time the offence was committed was in charge of, and was responsible to, the company for the conduct of business of the company, as well as the company, shall be deemed to be guilty of the offence and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly: 

Provided that nothing contained in this sub-section shall render any such person liable to any punishment if he proves that the offence was committed without his knowledge and that he exercised all due diligence to prevent the commission of such offence. 

( _2_ ) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section ( _1_ ), where an offence under this Code has been committed by a company and it is proved that the offence has been committed with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of, any director, manager, secretary or other officer of the company, such director, manager, secretary or other officer shall also be deemed to be guilty of that offence and shall be liable to be proceeded against and punished accordingly. 

_Explanation_ .—For the purposes of this section,— 

( _a_ ) "company" means any body corporate and includes— 

( _i_ ) a firm; or 

( _ii_ ) a limited liability partnership registered under the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008; or 

6 of 2009. 

( _iii_ ) other association of individuals; and 

( _b_ ) "director" in relation to a firm means a partner in the firm. 

Composition of offences. 

**89.** ( _1_ ) Notwithstanding anything contained in the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, 2 of 1974. any offence punishable under this Code, not being an offence punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and also with fine, may, on an application of the accused person, either before or after the institution of any prosecution, be compounded by a Gazetted Officer, as the appropriate Government may, by notification, specify, for a sum of fifty per cent. of the maximum fine provided for such offence punishable with fine only and for a sum of seventy-five per cent. provided for such offence punishable with imprisonment for a term which is not more than one year or with fine, in the manner as may be prescribed:

Provided that such amount of composition shall be credited to the Social Security Fund established under section 141 of the Social Security Code, 2020. 

( _2_ ) Nothing contained in sub-section ( _1_ ) shall apply to an offence committed by a person for the second time or thereafter within a period of three years from the date— 

( _a_ ) of commission of a similar offence which was earlier compounded; 

( _b_ ) of commission of similar offence for which such person was earlier convicted. 

( _3_ ) Every officer referred to in sub-section ( _1_ ) shall exercise the powers to compound an offence, subject to the direction, control and supervision of the appropriate Government. 

( _4_ ) Every application for the compounding of an offence shall be made in such manner as may be prescribed. 

( _5_ ) Where any offence is compounded before the institution of any prosecution, no prosecution shall be instituted in relation to such offence, against the offender in relation to whom the offence is so compounded. 

( _6_ ) Where the composition of any offence is made after the institution of any prosecution, such composition shall be brought by the officer referred to in sub-section ( _1_ ) in writing, to the notice of the adjudicating officer appointed under sub-section ( _1_ ) of section 85 before whom the prosecution is pending and on such notice of the composition of the offence being given, the person against whom the offence is so compounded shall be discharged. 

( _7_ ) Any person who fails to comply with an order made by the officer referred to in sub-section ( _1_ ), shall be liable to pay a sum equivalent to twenty per cent. of the maximum fine provided for the offence, in addition to such fine. 

( _8_ ) No offence punishable under the provisions of this Code shall be compounded except under and in accordance with the provisions of this section.

## UNFAIR LABOUR PRACTICES 

- I. ON THE PART OF EMPLOYERS AND TRADE UNIONS OF EMPLOYERS 

- (1) To interfere with, restrain from, or coerce, workers in the exercise of their right to organise, form, join or assist a Trade Union or to engage in concerted activities for the purposes of collective bargaining or other mutual aid or protection, that is to say,—

- (c) granting wage increase to workers at crucial periods of Trade Union organisation, with a view to undermining the efforts of the Trade Union organisation. 

- (2) To dominate, interfere with or contribute support, financial or otherwise, to any Trade Union, that is to say,— 

- (a) an employer taking an active interest in organising a Trade Union of his workers; and

- (3) To establish employer sponsored Trade Unions of workers. 

- (4) To encourage or discourage membership in any Trade Union by discriminating against any worker, that is to say,— 

- (a) discharging or punishing a worker, because he urged other workers to join or organise a Trade Union;

**3.** ( _1_ ) No promoter shall advertise, market, book, sell or offer for sale, or invite persons to purchase in any manner any plot, apartment or building, as the case may be, in any real estate project or part of it, in any planning area, without registering the real estate project with the Real Estate Regulatory Authority established under this Act:

## PART A - What your uploaded PDF actually is

The PDF is the "Alphabetical List of Central Acts": 891 entries, A to W. For each Act it gives only three things: the name, the year and the Act number. It does not contain the text of any rule, so the detailed rules below come from the Acts themselves.

How to read it:
- An asterisk (*) means a Spent Act, one that has served its purpose (for example the Defence of India Act 1962, Essential Service Maintenance Act 1968).
- Footnotes mark Acts struck down by the Supreme Court: the Illegal Migrants (Determination by Tribunals) Act 1983 (Sarbananda Sonowal, 2006), the National Tax Tribunal Act 2005 (Madras Bar Association, 2014), and the National Judicial Appointments Commission Act 2014 (SC Advocates-on-Record Association, 2015). Section 32-A of the NDPS Act is also declared unconstitutional to the extent it bars courts from suspending sentences.
- The "Act No." column is unreliable in this scan. Page numbers have merged into it (for example "Minimum Wages Act 1948 1112" really means Act 11 plus page 12). Use the year and name, and check the number on India Code.
- Some entries have OCR typos (for example "Nagal and" for Nagaland, "Ben chat" for Bench at). Treat names with care.
- The PDF is partly outdated. Examples: the Tribunals Reforms Act 2021 and the Bankers' Books Evidence Act 1891 have been replaced in 2026. The Income-tax Act 1961 has been replaced by the Income-tax Act 2025.
- The PDF has no entry for the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, the Income-tax Act 2025, the Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, the SHANTI Act 2025 and others added after it was printed. These appear below as "not in PDF".

## PART B - The 50 laws

### A. Criminal justice and courts

**1. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023** (PDF S.No. 79)
- Replaced the Indian Penal Code 1860. In force since 1 July 2024.
- New offences: organised crime, petty organised crime, terrorism, mob lynching (murder by five or more persons on grounds such as race, caste or religion), snatching, and acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India (replacing the old sedition section).
- Introduces community service as a punishment for petty offences. Adds offences against women and children, including sexual intercourse by deceitful means or false promise of marriage.

**2. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023** (S.No. 78)
- Replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure 1973. In force since 1 July 2024.
- Zero FIR (file at any police station), e-FIR, and mandatory audio-video recording of search and seizure.
- Forensic team visit for serious offences (punishment of 7 years or more).
- Fixed timelines: charge-sheet generally within 90 days, judgment within 45 days after trial ends, and framing of charges within 60 days.
- Trial in absentia for proclaimed offenders; attachment and forfeiture of proceeds of crime.

**3. Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023** (S.No. 80)
- Replaced the Indian Evidence Act 1872. In force since 1 July 2024.
- Electronic and digital records get the same status as paper documents. Covers emails, server logs, messages, location data and similar material.
- Adds secondary evidence rules for digital records and expands the definition of "document".

**4. Mediation Act, 2023** (S.No. 481)
- Creates a legal framework for pre-litigation mediation in civil and commercial disputes.
- Mediation should generally finish within 180 days (extendable by up to 180 days). A mediated settlement agreement is enforceable like a court decree.
- Sets up the Mediation Council of India and recognises community mediation. Online mediation is permitted.

**5. Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022** (S.No. 204)
- Replaced the Identification of Prisoners Act 1920.
- Allows police and prison officers to collect fingerprints, palm prints, iris and retina scans, photographs, biological samples and behavioural attributes from convicts and certain arrested or detained persons.
- Records are held by the NCRB and retained for up to 75 years, with provisions for deletion in some cases.

**6. Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024** (S.No. 689)
- Targets paper leaks and cheating in central recruitment and entrance exams (UPSC, SSC, RRB, IBPS, NTA and central ministries).
- Penalty: 3 to 5 years for unfair means, and 5 to 10 years with a fine of at least Rs 1 crore for organised paper-leak offences. Offences are cognisable, non-bailable and non-compoundable.
- Candidates themselves are generally not targeted for minor offences unless involved in the unfair means.

**7. Tribunals Reforms Act, 2026** (assent 13 August 2026; PDF still lists the 2021 Act, S.No. 850)
- New framework to improve the efficiency, independence, transparency and uniformity of tribunals. Check the Gazette text for tenure, qualification and appointment rules.

**8. Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2026** (assent 11 August 2026; PDF S.No. 824 is the 1956 Act)
- Raises the sanctioned strength of the Supreme Court from 34 to 38 judges including the Chief Justice of India.

**9. Maritime Anti-Piracy Act, 2022** (S.No. 508)
- Implements UNCLOS provisions on piracy and applies beyond India's territorial waters.
- Punishments up to life imprisonment, and death if piracy causes death. Allows Indian authorities to seize pirate vessels and arrest suspects.

**10. Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023** (not in PDF; amends many listed Acts)
- Decriminalised or reduced penalties in about 180 provisions across roughly 42 central Acts (for example the Information Technology Act, Drugs and Cosmetics Act, Environment Protection Act) to improve ease of doing business.
- Replaces many jail terms with monetary penalties.

### B. Elections, governance and national symbols

**11. Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023** (S.No. 150)
- Appointments are made by the President on the recommendation of a Selection Committee: the Prime Minister, the Leader of Opposition (or largest opposition party leader) in the Lok Sabha, and a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the PM.
- Six-year term (or until age 65). CEC and ECs have salary and status equal to a Supreme Court judge.

**12. Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Act, 2026** (assent in August 2026; original Act S.No. 663)
- Extends the penal provisions to cover the National Song (Vande Mataram) in addition to the National Anthem and the Flag.

**13. Kerala (Alteration of Name) Act, 2026** (assent 14 August 2026)
- Officially renames the State of Kerala as "Keralam".

**14. Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Act, 2026** (original Act S.No. 723)
- Amends Section 13 of the 1969 Act (see the Gazette for the exact change on delayed registration).

**15. Inter-Services Organisations (Command, Control and Discipline) Act, 2023** (S.No. 430)
- Lets the head of a joint or inter-service organisation exercise command, control and disciplinary power over personnel of all services posted there, enabling theatre commands.

**16. Repealing and Amending Act, 2023** (S.No. 739)
- Repeals obsolete and redundant laws and corrects minor errors, as part of the continuing clean-up of the statute book. Several older Acts in the PDF are of this kind.

### C. Digital, telecom and media

**17. Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023** (not in PDF)
- Applies to processing of digital personal data. Rights: consent, correction, erasure, grievance redressal and nomination.
- Data Fiduciaries must give notice, collect only needed data, and report breaches. Penalties can reach Rs 250 crore. Created the Data Protection Board of India.
- Rules were notified in late 2025 with phased implementation. Check the current rollout dates.

**18. Telecommunications Act, 2023** (S.No. 833)
- Replaced the Indian Telegraph Act 1885 and Wireless Telegraphy Act 1933.
- Spectrum assigned by auction, with administrative allocation for listed public-interest uses. Authorisation regime for telecom services.
- Lets the government intercept messages on grounds such as public safety, with safeguards. Requires verifiable identity for SIM users. Provides for right of way for telecom infrastructure.

**19. Post Office Act, 2023** (S.No. 643)
- Replaced the Indian Post Office Act 1898 and removed the Post Office's exclusive privilege to carry letters.
- Allows the Director General to set rules; includes limited interception powers on public-safety grounds and retains offences to protect postal services.

**20. Press and Registration of Periodicals Act, 2023** (S.No. 654)
- Replaced the 1867 Act. Online registration of periodicals through the Press Registrar General. A Press and Registration Appellate Board hears appeals.
- Digital news is outside its scope. Registration may be refused to those convicted of terrorist or unlawful activities.

**21. Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025** (not in PDF)
- Prohibits online money games (including advertising and financial transactions for them) and promotes esports and social/educational games.
- Provides for a regulatory authority and criminal penalties for offering real-money games.

**22. Cinematograph (Amendment) Act, 2023** (original Cinematograph Act 1952, S.No. 156)
- Introduces film piracy penalties (jail up to 3 years and fine), and splits the UA certificate into UA 7+, UA 13+ and UA 16+. Certificates are valid in perpetuity.

### D. Tax, finance and business

**23. Income-tax Act, 2025** (not in PDF; replaces Income-tax Act 1961, S.No. 364)
- Came into force on 1 April 2026. Simplified and restructured language, with the concept of "tax year" replacing "previous year" and "assessment year".
- Retains the main rules of the 1961 Act in a more streamlined form.

**24. Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026** (assent 17 August 2026)
- Amends the Income-tax Act 2025, the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2007 and the Finance Act 2026. Reported aims include attracting foreign capital and promoting domestic electronics manufacturing.

**25. Provisional Collection of Taxes Act, 2023** (S.No. 684)
- Allows certain tax proposals in the Budget to take effect immediately from the date of presentation for a limited period until the Finance Bill is enacted.

**26. Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025** (not in PDF)
- Raises FDI in Indian insurance companies from 74% to 100%, lowers net-owned-fund requirements for foreign reinsurers and gives IRDAI additional powers.

**27. Banking Laws (Amendment) Act, 2025** (amends Banking Regulation Act 1949, S.No. 64, and others)
- Allows up to four nominees for deposits and safe custody. Raises the "substantial interest" threshold in banks. Extends the tenure of directors of cooperative banks. Revises how unclaimed shares and dividends are transferred.

**28. Payment and Settlement Systems (Amendment) Act, 2026** (original Act 2007, S.No. 621)
- Passed in the Monsoon Session 2026 and assented on 17 August 2026. See the Gazette for details.

**29. Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 2026** (assent 13 August 2026; PDF lists the 1891 Act, S.No. 61)
- Replaces the 1891 law on using bankers' books as evidence in legal proceedings.

**30. Competition (Amendment) Act, 2023** (original S.No. 192)
- Adds a deal-value threshold (Rs 2,000 crore) for merger notification, settlement and commitment mechanisms, and penalties based on global turnover.

**31. National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development (NaBFID) Act, 2021** (S.No. 539)
- Creates a development finance institution for long-term infrastructure financing with government support and initial capital.

**32. The four Labour Codes** (Code on Wages 2019 and Code on Social Security 2020 are S.No. 176 and 175; Industrial Relations Code 2020 and Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020 are not in the PDF)
- Consolidate roughly 29 older labour laws. The Government brought the codes into force in November 2025.
- Key rules: statutory floor wage and timely wage payment, fixed-term employment with pro-rata benefits, social security for gig and platform workers (aggregator contribution), a wider ESI/EPF reach, and women allowed night shifts with consent and safety conditions.

### E. Energy, environment and infrastructure

**33. SHANTI Act, 2025** (Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India; not in PDF)
- Opens the nuclear power sector to private participation under licensing. Reported to repeal or replace the Atomic Energy Act 1962 (S.No. 56) and the Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act 2010 (S.No. 162). Verify the final text for liability caps and licence rules.

**34. Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022** (original S.No. 278)
- Creates a carbon credit trading scheme, makes renewable energy consumption obligations possible, and sets energy-efficiency norms for buildings and vehicles.

**35. Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023** (original S.No. 89)
- Decriminalises offences, exempts AYUSH practitioners and codified traditional knowledge from some benefit-sharing, and eases rules for Indian companies and researchers.

**36. Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980** (S.No. 873; renamed from Forest (Conservation) Act by the 2023 amendment)
- Requires central approval before forest land is used for non-forest purposes. The 2023 amendment exempts some categories (for example linear projects up to 100 km from borders and small security infrastructure) and has been challenged in the Supreme Court.

**37. Dam Safety Act, 2021** (S.No. 214)
- Applies to specified dams over 15 m (or 10-15 m with design conditions). Creates the National Committee on Dam Safety and the National Dam Safety Authority, plus State Dam Safety Organisations. Failure to comply can lead to imprisonment.

**38. Commission for Air Quality Management in NCR and Adjoining Areas Act, 2021** (S.No. 186)
- Creates a statutory commission with powers to issue binding directions on pollution sources in Delhi-NCR. Penalties up to 5 years and Rs 1 crore.

**39. Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025** (original S.No. 253)
- Strengthens disaster planning, gives statutory status to the National Crisis Management Committee and High Level Committee, and enables urban disaster management authorities.

### F. Transport, aviation, shipping and borders

**40. Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam, 2024** (S.No. 81)
- Replaced the Aircraft Act 1934. Covers design, manufacture, maintenance, ownership and operation of aircraft, and strengthens safety and security rules. Several penal provisions are modernised.

**41. Merchant Shipping Act, 2025** (not in PDF; part of a maritime package)
- Replaces the Merchant Shipping Act 1958 (S.No. 516). The package also includes new Acts on carriage of goods by sea, bills of lading, coastal shipping and ports, replacing older laws such as the Indian Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1925 (S.No. 369), the Indian Bills of Lading Act 1856 (S.No. 368) and the Indian Ports Act 1908 (S.No. 384). Confirm exact titles and dates in the Gazette.

**42. Major Port Authorities Act, 2021** (S.No. 494)
- Replaces port trusts with Boards of Port Authorities with more autonomy, and allows commercial and public-private partnership models.

**43. Boilers Act, 2025** (S.No. 93)
- Replaces the Boilers Act 1923 and decriminalises many offences while keeping safety certification and inspection.

**44. Protection of Interests in Aircraft Objects Act, 2025** (S.No. 679)
- Gives legal effect to the Cape Town Convention on international interests in aircraft equipment, helping aircraft leasing and financing.

**45. Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025** (not in PDF)
- Consolidates the Foreigners Act 1946 (S.No. 313), the Passport (Entry into India) Act 1920 (S.No. 618), the Registration of Foreigners Act 1939 (S.No. 724) and the Immigration (Carriers' Liability) Act 2000 (S.No. 359).
- Entry and stay generally require a valid passport and visa. Provides for penalties for violations and reporting duties for institutions hosting foreigners. Check the Gazette for exact penalties.

### G. Social, health, welfare and consumer

**46. Viksit Bharat-Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025 (VB-G RAM G)** (not in PDF; replaces MGNREGA 2005, S.No. 490)
- Passed 18-19 December 2025. Raises the statutory rural employment guarantee from 100 to 125 days per household per year.
- Changes funding patterns and planning structures. State governments must notify implementing schemes within six months of the Act coming into force.

**47. Waqf laws: Unified Waqf Management, Empowerment, Efficiency and Development Act, 1995 (as amended in 2025) and Mussalman Wakf (Repeal) Act, 2025** (S.No. 854 and S.No. 533)
- The 2025 amendment renamed the 1995 Act and changed waqf registration, survey, board composition and tribunal processes. The Mussalman Wakf Act 1923 was repealed.
- Parts of the amendment were challenged in the Supreme Court, which has passed interim orders. Check the latest status before relying on any provision.

**48. Surrogacy (Regulation) Act, 2021 and Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021** (S.No. 825 and 55)
- Surrogacy: only altruistic surrogacy, no commercial surrogacy. The intending couple must be legally married and meet age and eligibility criteria; the surrogate must be a married woman with a child and within a set age range. Commercial surrogacy is punishable with up to 10 years and a fine up to Rs 10 lakh.
- ART: regulates ART clinics and gamete banks, with registration, donor limits and a ban on sex selection.

**49. Health-profession commissions: National Medical Commission Act, 2019; National Dental Commission Act, 2023; National Nursing and Midwifery Commission Act, 2023** (S.No. 576, 560, 577)
- Replace the Medical Council of India, Dentists Act 1948 and Indian Nursing Council Act 1947. They set standards, regulate education, create licensing or exit exams (such as NExT for medicine) and form separate boards for ethics, registration and ratings.

**50. Consumer Protection Act, 2019** (S.No. 195)
- Creates the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) with powers to recall goods, order refunds and penalise misleading ads and endorsers.
- Adds product liability, e-commerce duties and mediation. District, State and National Commissions are set by value of the claim.
